Dear Editors,

We submit our manuscript, ``\papertitle,'' for consideration as a Practice of
Epidemiology article in the \textit{American Journal of Epidemiology}'s special
collection ``Artificial Intelligence in Health: From Pattern Discovery to
Population Inference.''

Health departments act on influenza neighborhood by neighborhood, but most
forecasts cover states or regions. We forecast emergency department visits for
influenza-like illness 1, 2, and 4 weeks ahead in Boston's 14 neighborhoods and
repeated the analysis in Columbus and Chicago. Our graph neural network uses
each neighborhood's recent trajectory, weather, demographic measures, and a map
of neighboring areas. On seasons not used in design, it had 18\% to 28\% less
error than persistence in Boston. Removing one input at a time showed that the
map accounted for this gain; weather and demographics did not.

We think the work will interest the journal's readers because it tests, on
held-out seasons and against equally tuned comparison models, whether
neighborhood forecasts are accurate enough to guide local public health action,
and which information makes them so. The models are released as an open-source
package that a health department can run on a laptop.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration
elsewhere. All authors have approved it, and none has a conflict of interest to
declare. If the manuscript is accepted, we intend to publish open access, with
article processing charges covered through Northeastern University Library's
agreement with Oxford University Press.
