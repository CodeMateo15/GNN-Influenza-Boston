\section*{Data Availability}

Boston neighborhood influenza-like illness rates are published by the Boston
Public Health Commission. Chicago ZIP code influenza-like illness activity
levels are published by the Chicago Department of Public Health on the City of
Chicago Data Portal (dataset 8vvr-jv2g; ZIP code boundaries, dataset
unjd-c2ca). Columbus data
were provided by Columbus Public Health as de-identified visit-level extracts;
with their agreement, the weekly area-level counts used in every analysis are
publicly available with the code \NOTE{confirm Columbus Public Health's terms
permit releasing the weekly counts}, and the visit-level extracts are available
from Columbus Public Health on request. Weather data come from the Open-Meteo
archive (CC BY 4.0), and boundary files from the City of Boston, the City of
Chicago, and the US Census Bureau. All code, frozen model
configurations, and the scripts that produce every table and figure are
available at \url{https://github.com/CodeMateo15/GNN-Influenza-Boston}
\NOTE{tag the release used for this paper, e.g., paper-v1, and cite it or a
Zenodo DOI here}.

The forecasting models are also released as an open-source Python package for
health departments, areaFluForecast, version 1.1.0 (MIT license; archived at
Zenodo, \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23350130}; all versions,
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23327108}). It installs with
\texttt{pip install areaFluForecast} (\url{https://pypi.org/project/areaFluForecast/}),
runs on a laptop CPU, and is documented at
\url{https://areafluforecast.readthedocs.io}; source code is at
\url{https://github.com/CodeMateo15/areaFluForecast}. From a department's weekly
area-level rates and boundary file, it builds the neighborhood map, scores the
graph neural network, tuned LSTM, Cola-GNN, and seasonal ARIMA against
persistence on the department's own past seasons, and then forecasts 1 to 4
weeks ahead with FluSight-style quantiles. Pretrained Boston models are
included for cities with short histories. In a parity check on Explorer
(October 2026), the package reproduced the paper's Boston evaluation: 23 of 27
model, season, and horizon results were numerically identical, and the other
4 differed by at most 0.4\% in mean WIS.

Real-time forecasts for the 2026--2027 season, made each week with the same
models, are posted on the Boston Flu Outlook dashboard
(\NOTE{Posit Connect Cloud URL}; data and source,
\url{https://github.com/CodeMateo15/boston-flu-outlook}), which also shows
Chicago. These are experimental research forecasts, not an official Boston
Public Health Commission product, and are not part of the evaluation reported
in this paper: unlike the evaluated models, they are trained on every
available week.
